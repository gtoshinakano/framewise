Built 2026-11-22. Code: `backend/app/voice_resolver.py` (phrases, signals, the pure `resolve`, writing, undo, escalations,
answers, the frame sheet, the job), routes in `backend/app/voice_resolver_api.py`, job kind `voice_resolver` (**audio

## What an open voice looks like (2026-11-22)

- **Episodes 4-7** were transcribed before line records: one row per word, no voice scores saved, and the user linked
  whole diarization labels to characters. Their open voices are whole labels nobody linked (Episode 6: SPEAKER_04 69
  rows, SPEAKER_06 55, SPEAKER_01 33, SPEAKER_00 15, plus 69 unlabelled word fragments).
- **Episodes 1-3** use line records with per-line voice scores and Qwen attribution; one open line each.
- Burned-in subtitles are Portuguese and carry no name tags, so the name-tag signal is wired but silent on this project.
- The project's voice samples were all cut from Episodes 1-2.

## How it decides

1. **Phrases**: consecutive open lines of one label with gaps ≤ 0.5 s and no other line between (≤ 12 s).
2. **Signals** per phrase and per label (a label is one voice):
   - `voice`: the phrase's audio (or, under 0.6 s, the silence around it too, which then needs a score of 0.5) and the
     label's joined audio (up to 30 s of its longest phrases) scored against the samples (`line_voices.score_clip`),
     named at 0.35 with a 0.05 lead. Without samples or audio: the scores transcription saved on line records.
   - `label`: the label's other lines already linked, ≥ 5 lines and ≥ 80% one character.
   - `picture`: exactly one character `speaking: yes` in the keyframe segments over the phrase, else in the cut's
     video analysis (direction contract). The label's picture is the majority (≥ 2 votes, ≥ 60%) of its phrases.
   - `name_tag`: a `(名前)`/`[名前]` tag in an overlapping subtitle naming one character.
   - A given or full glossary name the phrase says excludes that character (a family name never: relatives share it).
3. **Families**: voice and label are both *sound* (the diarizer clusters the same embeddings); picture and tag are
   others. **Apply** when two families name one character and no signal names another, in this order: the whole label,
   single phrases, then the label's remaining phrases when ≥ 3 of its phrases were settled and all as one character
   (consensus, tier `rules`).
4. **Ask** the rest, one escalation per label. Unlabelled fragments with no signal at all are left alone (diarization
   noise between two voices); the others are grouped by their candidates so one answer settles them. **Claude** gets a
   voice when its cuts show people and the run's Claude budget lasts (frame sheet: three frames over each sample phrase,
   spread over at least 0.6 s). **The person** gets a label whose joined voice resembles no sample (top < 0.25: it may
   be a new character), and what Claude is unsure of. A voice the person left unknown is never asked again.
5. **Writing**: one revision per line with the character, the source's `origin` kept, `speaker_source='agent'`,
   `confidence='high'`, `evidence_json.agent` with the signals; cuts stale with "Speaker changed"; one `agent_decisions`
   row per unit (kind `voices`). Speaker attribution never re-decides an `agent` line. A person's answer is written like
   the app's own speaker edits (`manual_edit`, `speaker_source='manual'`). Lines inside a sequence are left alone.
   After applying, speaker attribution is queued again when open line records remain.

The profile's mode `ask` turns every apply into a question for the person ("The agent would give it to …"); `off`
queues nothing. **The project's stored profile has voices on Ask me**, so a run there applies nothing until it is
switched to Apply in the Autopilot profile dialog.

## Replay (2026-11-22, computed voice scores, CPU of the audio image)

`REPLAY_IMAGE=framewise-audio-rocm:local autopilot/replay/run.sh voices --scores computed --episodes "Episode 2,Episode 4,Episode 5,Episode 6,Episode 7"`
(5 minutes). Each label the user linked (≥ 95% one character) is hidden in turn and decided with everything else as it is.

| | lines right | lines wrong | precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| whole label | 8,709 | 0 | 100% |
| single phrase | 83 | 7 | 92% |
| consensus | 10 | 0 | 100% |
| **all applied** | **8,802** | **7** | **99.9%** |

3,107 hidden lines were asked instead of applied, 264 left (inside sequences). The 7 wrong lines are one phrase,
"見たわけじゃないからな。", inside the label the user linked to Kaede (Episode 7 SPEAKER_05): masculine speech that the voice
scores as Sakuta, so the reference itself is probably wrong (a diarizer cluster with one stray Sakuta line). Before the
0.5 bar for padded short phrases, a 0.4 s "何？" was also misattributed.

Every hidden label's joined voice named its true character first (23/23; lowest margin Tomoe 0.671 vs Shoko 0.616 in Episode 4).
**Episode 4 got no picture signal** (its video analyses are an older format without per-character `speaking`), so all
four of its labels were asked although the voice alone was right with margins of 0.06-0.40. Hand-set lines (Episodes
1-3) are too few to score: 7 sit inside sequences, 2 were asked.

## Decisions for the user

1. **Voices on Apply.** The stored profile has `voices: ask`. Switching it to Apply lets the label-level decisions
   (100% in the replay) be written.
2. **A strong voice alone?** Episode 4 (and any episode whose pictures were analysed before `speaking` existed) can only
   be settled by voice. A rule "a label's joined voice ≥ 0.75 with a 0.2 lead is enough" would have been right on every
   hidden label that reaches it (16 of 23), but it departs from section 5's "two independent signals". Not built; the
   alternative is re-running visual analysis on Episode 4.
