## How it decides (`plan_line`)

A line crosses a hard cut when it lasts more than 0.15 s on both sides of it. For each crossing, in this order:

1. **Audio trim.** When `audio.wav` stays quiet from the cut to the line's end (or from its start to the cut), the crossing
   part is the aligner's padding: the edge moves to where the voice last sounds before the cut (first sounds after it),
   0.06 s into the silence, never past the cut. Word times that no longer fit inside the new edges are dropped, as a manual
   retime does.
2. **Split.** When words lie on both sides of the cut: the quiet stretches of at least 0.3 s (`sequence_timeline.find_pauses`
   on the line's audio ±1 s) that fall between two word midpoints, at a place a phrase can end, within 1.5 s of the cut.
   The nearest wins. The split sits on the cut when the cut is inside the quiet stretch, else 0.06 s into the stretch on
   the cut's side. The words divide by midpoint; the two texts together must spell the line exactly (`transcript_parts.parts`
   spreads the line's own text over the word slots, so an `srt_edit` respelling keeps every character).
   - *A place a phrase can end* (`natural`): after punctuation always; otherwise never when the left part ends on a small
     っ or the right part starts with a particle or auxiliary token (`NO_START`). The aligner gives morphemes, and a
     pause between `わかっ` and `た`, or before `んだろう`, is not a phrase boundary.
3. **Word trim.** When every word lies on one side, the edge moves to where the voice stops after the last word
   (`speech_edge`), unless a word itself runs across the cut.
4. **Leave** otherwise: no usable pause (the phrase bleeds into the next cut, which is acceptable, D4), no word timings,
   or a text rewritten since its words were aligned (similarity under 0.6).

The job applies the plans in its publish transaction; a piece that still crosses another cut is planned again (up to 4
passes). Lines inside a sequence's range are left alone and counted: editing them would make the user's confirmed
dialogue stale.

**Origin (D17):** split and trimmed pieces keep the source line's `origin` (`model`, `manual_edit`, `srt_edit`): the words
are still the model's or the user's, so re-transcription and speaker attribution treat them exactly as before. Provenance
and undo are the `agent_decisions` row (kind `line_splits`, target the original line, `after.rows` the pieces). Undo writes a
copy of the original line that supersedes the pieces, while they are current and unchanged. A person's edit of a piece
(`PUT`, delete, merge, split routes) marks the decision overridden (`note_override` now also matches ids named in a
decision's `after`).
