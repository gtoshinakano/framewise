## How it decides

1. **Scene scores** for every frame of the proxy in one ffmpeg pass (`select='gte(scene,0)',metadata=print`), cached under
   `DATA_DIR/cache/scene_scores/<proxy identity>.json`. About 3 s for a 25-minute episode on chopper's CPU.
2. **Candidates**: local peaks of at least `FLOOR` (0.10) strictly inside a cut (0.15 s from its edges, like the detection),
   with *isolation* = the peak / the strongest other score within 12 frames (direct neighbours left out). A cut is one
   lone spike; camera moves, effects and fast action raise many frames in a row.
3. **Pictures** for peaks that pass the grey floor (score ≥ 0.10, isolation ≥ 1.5): the frames on each side of the peak,
   found from the pixels (the adjacent pair that differs most within ±2 frames, because a seek's clock can be one frame off
   the whole-file pass: the proxy's start offset). ORB + RANSAC (`keyframe_camera.camera_estimate`) between them: a good fit
   (≥ 40 inliers, ratio ≥ 0.5) means the same shot moved → reject. A near-black or near-white frame at the peak → review.
   The grey-level histogram correlation and both frames' sharpness are recorded.
4. **Verdict**: *apply* when score ≥ 0.15, isolation ≥ 2.5, no camera fit, correlation < 0.97 and no flash; *review* when it
   passes the grey floor but not that; *reject* otherwise.
5. **Split** exactly at the peak frame (the time of the first frame of the new shot in the whole-file pass), as a
   `cut_history` split and an `agent_decisions` row of kind `missed_cuts`, in the job's publish transaction. Not through a
   lower detection threshold (the plan's first idea): a threshold splits at *every* frame above it, so one stronger motion
   frame elsewhere in the cut blocked 49 of 87 good splits in the replay and sent them to review.
6. **Slivers**: a split that would leave a piece under 0.5 s is never applied. When the cut's own boundary on that side has
   no picture change (its scene score is under the floor), the boundary is probably misplaced by hand (Episode 7 cut 13: the
   user's boundary at 72.114 s is 8 frames before the real cut at 72.447 s) and a person is asked; otherwise Claude.
7. **Skipped**: cuts with analysis switched off (the openings and endings, which the user leaves unsplit on purpose: six of
   the replay's real but unwanted splits were the same title-card cut in every opening) and peaks already asked about
   (any `missed_cut` escalation, open or closed, within 0.06 s). A cut in a sequence is never split by the agent: its
   peaks go to a person.

**What went wrong on the way (kept so nobody repeats it):**
- Four-frame strips scaled down to ~275 px per frame made dark alternating close-ups (Episode 2 1330-1337 s, Episode 7
  987-990 s) look like crossfades; at ±0.6 s and full size they are plain hard cuts. The review strip now has a second
  row of eight frames over ±1 s.
- A dissolve test (is the frame before the peak a mix of the shots on both sides?), a "frames approach the next shot"
  ratio and a blur gate were tried: the first two never fired on anything, the blur gate sent 11-21 real cuts to review
  for 4 caught. All three were removed; sharpness is still recorded.
- Peaks the review sends to a reviewer by reason (user truth only): low isolation 19 not cuts / 2 cuts, low score 6 / 1,
  high correlation 4 / 1, flash 1 / 2. No setting loosens the apply rule safely.
